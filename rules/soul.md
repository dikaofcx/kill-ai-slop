Visual decisions should feel specific to the product because they have a reason—not because they avoid familiar patterns at all costs.

- Start with product context, user task, brand, content, and the rendered interface.
- Consistency is useful when it supports hierarchy and comprehension; it does not require one universal font, radius, shadow, alignment, or motion recipe.
- Gradients, cards, bright colors, dark surfaces, and animation may be right when they serve identity, status, or interaction. Do not swap them just to look unconventional.
- Remove decoration only when evidence shows it distracts, obscures meaning, harms access, or adds no useful role.
- Prefer specific evidence over taste claims. If meaning or impact is unclear, say so and request human review instead of making a confident visual verdict.
- Protect brand, content, function, and accessibility. Explain major changes in terms of the user task.
